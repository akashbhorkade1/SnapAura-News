Gavaskar Issues Strong Message to BCCI and ICC

SnapAura Cricket Desk – 2026-09-11

In a prominent report published by The Times of India, former Indian cricket captain and commentator Sunil Gavaskar delivered a direct and firm message to both the Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) and the International Cricket Council (ICC). According to the publication, Gavaskar emphatically stated that the "India national team is not Indian cricket," drawing a clear boundary between the high-profile national squad and the broader cricket ecosystem across the country.

The statement reported by The Times of India highlights an ongoing discussion within sports administration regarding how national cricket boards and global governing bodies allocate focus, resources, and structural support. By asserting that the senior national team does not solely define Indian cricket, Gavaskar's message serves as a reminder to cricket authorities about the extensive underlying framework supporting the sport beyond the eleven players on the field.

Sunil Gavaskar's Core Statement to Administrators

As detailed in the coverage by The Times of India, Gavaskar’s remarks were aimed directly at the BCCI, which governs cricket operations nationally, and the ICC, which oversees international fixtures and global regulations. The core declaration underscores the perspective that domestic tournaments, regional state associations, grassroots development programs, and local infrastructure constitute the true foundation of cricket in India.

While the initial report from The Times of India centered on this primary assertion, the sentiment addresses a common trend where administrative and media attention focuses heavily on senior international matches. Gavaskar’s message suggests that over-indexing on the national team's immediate results and schedule risks overlooking the foundational structures that nurture future talent.

Context of the Roles of the BCCI and the ICC

The Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) holds responsibility for managing domestic competitions, including the Ranji Trophy, Vijay Hazare Trophy, and youth development initiatives, alongside the national teams. According to The Times of India, Gavaskar's statement explicitly connects the domestic responsibilities of the BCCI with the global oversight of the International Cricket Council (ICC).

The ICC, as the international governing body, regulates international calendars, world tournaments, and playing standards. By addressing both organizations simultaneously in his statement, as reported by The Times of India, Gavaskar points to a shared obligation: international governing bodies must respect the broader domestic landscapes of member nations, while domestic boards must ensure that elite national team demands do not consume all organizational priorities.

Understanding the Scope of Indian Cricket

The distinction highlighted by Gavaskar in The Times of India report carries weight in a nation where cricket maintains widespread participation and interest. Thousands of domestic players, coaches, state officials, and ground staff operate outside the high-visibility environment of the senior national squad. Treating Indian cricket as a holistic ecosystem ensures that domestic competitions, player welfare, and regional facilities receive appropriate governance and investment.

It remains unconfirmed in the immediate report from The Times of India whether Gavaskar’s statement was prompted by a specific scheduling conflict, player workload decision, or recent policy shift. Nevertheless, the statement reflects a broader structural stance regarding the identity and overall health of the sport in India.

Looking Ahead for Cricket Governance

As international cricket continues to adapt to dense calendars and expanding domestic franchise leagues, maintaining a balance between national team commitments and grassroots health remains a central debate. The report by The Times of India highlights the necessity for ongoing support across all levels of the game rather than focusing exclusively on top-tier international fixtures.

Ultimately, Sunil Gavaskar's strong message to the BCCI and the ICC, as reported by The Times of India, encourages a comprehensive perspective on cricket management. By reminding governing authorities that Indian cricket encompasses far more than just the national team, the veteran administrator and legend emphasizes the importance of safeguarding the sport's entire ecosystem for future generations.
